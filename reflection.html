Reflection — Data Engineering Internship, Optik Consultancy

What twelve weeks in industry
changed about how I work.

Between semesters I completed a twelve-week internship with Optik Consultancy, working alongside a cross-disciplinary team of engineers on an automated road-defect measurement system for our client, Damage Control Project Management (DCPM). This is a reflection on what I expected going in, what actually happened, and how it shifted the kind of engineer I want to be.

01 — Expectations

What were your expectations before you joined?

I expected the internship to give me a chance to apply the technical skills I'd built through my degree to a genuine, real-world problem. I also expected it to show me what it's actually like to work inside a professional engineering environment — collaborating with clients, supervisors and colleagues rather than just coursemates.

02 — Reality

What was the reality, and how did it differ?

The biggest gap between expectation and reality was how much weight was placed on understanding the client's problem before touching a solution. University projects almost always arrive with the problem, requirements and specifications already defined. Here, there was no such brief — our team spent a significant amount of time in conversation with the client, researching relevant technologies, and working out the best way to address the challenges they were actually facing, before any of that was settled.

03 — Key lessons

What were the most important lessons, and why?

The clearest lesson was the value of a genuinely cross-disciplinary team. Ours brought together data, software and civil engineers. I already understood the idea in principle, but this was the first time I saw how directly different perspectives improve a solution in practice — our combined knowledge let us account for considerations that no one of us would have caught alone, and produced a system that served the client's needs far better than any single-discipline approach could have.

04 — Value proposition

What's your value proposition to an employer now?

The clearest shift was in my ability to communicate complex technical ideas to non-technical stakeholders. DCPM didn't have deep technical background in the areas our project touched, so our team had to explain what we were building in clear, accessible language. Rather than describing how a system worked, we focused on the value it created — how it would change their existing workflow and address the specific challenges they'd raised. That's a skill I can now point to directly, not just claim.

05 — Looking ahead

How did the internship shape the roles you're interested in?

It sharpened my interest in consultancy-style work — I'd like to keep working closely with a client to apply emerging technology to problems that have real operational stakes. It also grew my interest in project management within cross-disciplinary teams; I want to keep learning how to draw out the specific strengths different specialists bring and turn that into a solution that actually fits the client's needs, rather than one that's simply technically impressive.

This experience is one of the things I draw on directly in my cover letter for the Industrus Engineering Graduate Program.
