Re: Engineering Graduate Program

Dear Sir or Madam,

I am writing to express my interest in the Industrus Engineering Graduate Program. I am a prospective graduate from the University of Technology Sydney, completing a Bachelor of Engineering majoring in Data Science. I recently completed a twelve-week internship as a data engineer at Optik Consultancy, working alongside a cross-disciplinary team of engineers to deliver an AI-powered solution for our client. I believe the combination of my technical background, practical industry experience and genuine interest in applying emerging technology to real problems makes me well suited to this graduate role.

3.1

A commitment to ethical conduct and the highest standards of professional accountability

During my internship I maintained a strong commitment to professional accountability, particularly through the project handover. I made sure the software was clearly communicated to the client through documentation, a video user guide and a walkthrough meeting, so they could confidently understand and use what we'd built. Throughout development we kept the client informed on project scope, our use of AI technologies, and any technical limitations or challenges as they arose. That transparency meant DCPM received not just a technically sound solution, but the documentation needed to develop and implement it further on their own.

3.2

Demonstrated ability to communicate with other engineers and stakeholders from different fields

I worked closely with a cross-disciplinary team of six engineers spanning software, civil and data engineering, as well as directly with our client, Damage Control Project Management (DCPM). As a civil infrastructure company, DCPM's expertise lies in infrastructure repair and project management rather than software or AI. We ran regular meetings and interviews with them to understand their existing workflow, the challenges they faced, and the outcomes they wanted from the project. As the team member with the strongest data engineering background, I was responsible for identifying emerging technologies and assessing their viability — which meant translating complex technical concepts into language that matched the client's background, and kept our solution aligned with what they actually needed.

3.3

The ability to engage with a creative, innovative and proactive environment

Road defect detection and measurement is a genuinely difficult problem in road assessment. Rather than relying on traditional photogrammetry or orthographic camera-based methods, our team developed a novel approach combining homography, machine learning and computer vision to automatically identify and measure road defects. This significantly reduced the manual effort in our client's inspection workflow and showed how emerging technology can be applied creatively to solve a practical engineering problem. I was primarily responsible for the homography and defect-segmentation pipelines — work that strengthened my instinct to look for an inventive technical approach rather than defaulting to an established one.

3.4

Demonstrated ability to use and manage information

My internship required me to interpret a large volume of information from our client, including their existing workflow, requirements, data and current software. Before we designed anything, we worked closely with DCPM to understand how their systems operated and where the limitations were. Using that information to guide our technical decisions let us build a solution that was not only effective, but integrated smoothly into their existing workflow — a direct demonstration of my ability to manage and organise large volumes of information to make sound technical decisions.

3.5

The ability to manage your own performance in a professional environment

I was responsible for a substantial share of the project's machine learning components, including automated segmentation and homography. To stay on track, I reviewed my progress regularly, maintained my tasks on our team's Trello board, and took part in daily check-ins. We also met with DCPM weekly via Microsoft Teams to give concise updates and raise any technical obstacles, questions or decisions that needed their input. As a result, I consistently delivered my sections on schedule and kept both my team and client well informed — experience that strengthened my ability to manage my own performance independently in a professional setting.

3.6

A demonstrated ability to work as part of a team and to show leadership when required

I've also built this through my university project work. In Professional Studio A, I was team leader for a software project developing an AI-powered photo enhancement application. I worked with the team to divide roles according to each person's skills, technical knowledge and interests, and we delivered a minimum viable product that achieved a High Distinction. That experience showed me how to work effectively as part of an engineering team, and how to recognise and draw on individual strengths to get to an effective product.
